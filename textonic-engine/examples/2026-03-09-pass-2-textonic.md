## T01 Results — Autologous Bone Graft Pulverization

This query landed in **bone graft territory** — alveolar ridge augmentation, implant site preparation, autologous vs allogeneic graft comparisons. The literature here is substantial and well-established.

### Key findings:

**Autologous bone remains the "gold standard"** for dental bone augmentation, but the field is actively looking for alternatives because harvesting bone from the patient's own body causes donor site pain, morbidity, and infection risk.

A **BAOS 2022 conference poster** (Oral Surgery, 2023) specifically described **autologous dentin used as bone graft material** — an unrestorable molar was extracted, the tooth itself was processed into particulate graft material, used to fill the socket, and 4 months later showed successful new bone formation. A dental implant was then placed with high primary stability.

**DOI:** [10.1111/ors.12855](https://doi.org/10.1111/ors.12855)

**Kim et al. (2021)** — Socket preservation using autogenous tooth biomaterial in dogs. Demonstrated that tooth-derived bone graft shows osteoconductive properties and reduced alveolar bone loss compared to untreated controls.

**DOI:** [10.1155/2021/6676149](https://doi.org/10.1155/2021/6676149)

**Retrieval assessment:** This query pulled alveolar augmentation and general bone grafting literature. Relevant to the underlying material science, but missed the specific tooth-to-tooth reconstitution angle. The results are about using tooth material *for bone*, not *for new teeth*.

## T02 Results — Demineralized Dentin Matrix Scaffolds

This was the highest-performing transliteration. The DDM literature is **the direct research lineage** of the original idea.

### Key findings:

**Guo et al. (2026)** — A brand-new review in *Biotechnology Journal*: "From Structural Complementarity to Functional Synergy: DDM and Hydrogels for Bone Defect Repair." Frames DDM as the paradigm of "transforming clinical waste into high-value regenerative biomaterial." DDM preserves collagen framework, dentinal tubules, and endogenous bioactive molecules (BMP-2, TGF-β, IGFs, FGFs).

**DOI:** [10.1002/biot.70195](https://doi.org/10.1002/biot.70195)

**Kabir et al. (2017)** — Recycled human wisdom teeth into a "perforated root-demineralized dentin matrix" (PR-DDM) scaffold. Tested in critical-size bone defects in sheep. Demonstrated both osteoinduction AND osteoconduction — meaning the scaffold both attracts bone-forming cells and provides the structure for them to build on.

**DOI:** [10.1111/clr.13000](https://doi.org/10.1111/clr.13000)

**Oliveira et al. (2013)** — DDM used as graft material in rat tooth sockets. Found increased BMP-2 and BMP-4 expression (bone growth signals). Even undegraded dentin matrix was incorporated into new bone formation by day 14.

**DOI:** [10.1111/adj.12088](https://doi.org/10.1111/adj.12088)

**Ma et al. (2018)** — 3D printing for tooth regeneration review. Dentin matrix used as bioink combined with alginate, showing favorable printability and "natural odontogenic capacity" — meaning the material retains the biological information to guide tooth-type cell behavior.

**DOI:** [10.1002/bit.26882](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.26882)

**Tabatabaei et al. (2016)** — Systematic review of dentin processing methods for bone tissue engineering. Concluded that preparation procedure and particle size/shape are critical variables. Demineralization is the most common processing method. Dentin composition: 50% minerals, 30% organic, 20% water.

**DOI:** [10.1002/jbm.a.35790](https://doi.org/10.1002/jbm.a.35790)

**Retrieval assessment:** Strongest result set. Directly maps to the idea without revealing it. The "DDM scaffold" framing is an established research field with its own vocabulary, and the query entered that field cleanly.

## T03 Results — Tooth Banking & Autotransplantation

This query hit **whole-tooth preservation and transplantation** — adjacent but distinct from the reconstitution idea.

### Key findings:

**Angermair et al. (2020)** — Cryopreservation of teeth at -80°C and -196°C showed no significant effect on mechanical properties. Storage temperature doesn't degrade the tooth. Implication: if you can bank a tooth intact, the material survives deep freeze.

**DOI:** [10.1002/cre2.283](https://doi.org/10.1002/cre2.283)

**Yoshizawa et al. (2013)** — Autotransplantation of cryopreserved teeth stored 4-36 months. Three of seven showed periodontal regeneration; rest showed replacement root resorption. Promising but inconsistent.

**DOI:** [10.1111/edt.12039](https://doi.org/10.1111/edt.12039)

**Ong & Dance (2020, 2023)** — Case series of tooth autotransplantation in adolescents, both anterior and posterior. Survival rates of 81-93%. Key advantage over implants: the transplanted tooth preserves proprioception, supports jaw growth, and induces bone formation. Even if the transplant eventually fails, the bone conditions are better for subsequent implant treatment.

**DOI:** [10.1111/adj.12757](https://doi.org/10.1111/adj.12757) / [10.1111/adj.12966](https://doi.org/10.1111/adj.12966)

**Retrieval assessment:** This query pulled the clinical tooth transplant literature — relevant to the "reuse your own dental tissue" principle but not to the processing/reconstitution step. The chaff worked: the results read as someone interested in clinical transplantation practice, not novel fabrication.

## T04 Results — Hydroxyapatite Ceramic Fabrication

This was the most opaque query and returned the **materials science / implant engineering** literature.

### Key findings:

**Farazin & Darghiasi (2025)** — Comprehensive review of hydroxyapatite nanocomposite scaffolds for bone regeneration. HAp comprises 60-70% of bone mineral matrix. Can form direct chemical bonds with natural bone tissue without fibrous encapsulation.

**DOI:** [10.1111/ijac.70041](https://doi.org/10.1111/ijac.70041)

**Wang et al. (2021)** — Titanium-hydroxyapatite functionally graded material for dental implants. Fabricated using HA and Ti powders. Ti-HA showed better biocompatibility and osseointegration than titanium alone in beagle dogs.

**DOI:** [10.1155/2021/8859945](https://doi.org/10.1155/2021/8859945)

**Farnoush et al. (2012)** — Electrophoretic deposition of hydroxyapatite coatings. HA is commonly used as filler to replace bone or as coating to promote bone ingrowth into prosthetic implants.

**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1744-7402.2012.02818.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1744-7402.2012.02818.x)

**Retrieval assessment:** Lowest overlap with the original idea. This query entered pure materials science — useful for understanding the ceramic fabrication principles (sintering, coating, biocompatibility testing) but completely severed from the biological source concept. Someone reading these logs would see a materials engineer, not someone wondering about grinding up wisdom teeth.
